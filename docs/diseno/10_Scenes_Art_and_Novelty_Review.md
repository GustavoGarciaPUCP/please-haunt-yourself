# PLEASE HAUNT YOURSELF — Scenes, art direction and novelty review

November 6, 2026 · Revised design direction after the participant's final critical review request · Documentation and concept art only.

## Verdict on the previous baseline

Document09 closed many behavioral contradictions. It did **not** yet justify calling the experience a beautiful, varied haunted operating system. It described one central appearance, a careful workshop around it, and two productions with substantially the same structure. A palette, six app names, error reports and more documentation do not establish an exciting experience.

The specific weaknesses were:

1. One camera-coverage gag was carrying too much of the proposed game.
2. Mail, rehearsal reports and saving mostly rewarded administrative correctness rather than a distinct scene.
3. Visual direction lacked composed images, character treatment and an escalation from an empty desktop to an inhabited one.

Creative and critical agents exchanged objections; a separate art-direction agent reviewed the visual promise. They agreed on these gaps. Their agreement is document critique, not player observation or evidence of originality.

**Revised recommendation:** retain the six applications, but organize the experience around **four playable tableaux**, with a brief boot and a saved-state premiere. UNINSTALL's voluntary farewell connects the tableaux; it is not advertised as a fifth puzzle. The six storyboard frames include opening and ending; they are not six levels or six puzzles.

## Authority and limits of this revision

09 remains authoritative for modes, genuine departure, replay checkpoints, immutable original recordings, visibility, recovery and completion. This document adds the scene compositions, CANVAS's visible extraction, POSTMAN's returned-view postcards and the art treatment of the premiere. These additions are explicit design changes, not implemented features. The application source, tests and existing Kiro specifications remain unchanged.

The later [11 — Build readiness and motion contract](11_Build_Readiness_and_Motion.md) specifies first-run routing, a declared shared admission object for the compatible one-decision branch, code-driven motion ordering and recovery. It recommends starting the existing scoped build rather than adding scenes. Installation of Codex skills in that review is not Kiro usage or evidence of rendered quality.

Four scenes share one fictional OS, its cast and show data. They do not require four unrelated game engines or four campaigns. Each scene needs a dominant visual object, a different operation and a result that persists elsewhere. A maximized inspector or a new wallpaper alone does not meet this definition.

## Novelty: what we can and cannot claim

Published descriptions checked on November6,2026; no hands-on play or video-based quality audit was performed in this review.

| Predecessor | Established by its own published description | Consequence for our claim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Outcore: Desktop Adventure](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1275670/Outcore_Desktop_Adventure/) | Uses Windows applications including Paint and Notepad in puzzles and a desktop-centered narrative | An OS, living desktop character or old application used in a puzzle is not our invention |
| [Windowkill](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2726450/Windowkill/) | Its shrinking game window and window edges are part of its shooter mechanic | Moving or manipulating window geometry cannot itself be sold as unprecedented |
| [KinitoPET](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2075070/KinitoPET/) | Advertises psychological horror through an early2000s virtual assistant and adaptive behavior | A haunted assistant or retro desktop horror is not sufficient differentiation |
| [Seance](https://devpost.com/software/seance), Best Costume Contest | Its submission describes folklore/history exploration through a19-country map and page-flipping questions | Clear purpose, coherent atmosphere and a finished presentation matter; six unusual app names are not an equivalent achievement |
| [SpookBnB](https://devpost.com/software/spookbnb-when-dark-mode-gets-truly-dark), Overall3rd | Rental interface transforms into horror via dark mode; its write-up lists four fragments but also says six rooms with one interactive bathroom puzzle in future-work discussion | Its published account is not a reliable basis for equating room count with distinct mechanics; the legible transformation is a useful reference, not a numerical quality score |

The direct Devpost pages returned403 in this review; the published submissions were available through indexed search results. Award and feature statements above are attributed to those submissions. This does not amount to playing their demos or confirming their implementation quality.

Our differentiation hypothesis is narrower: **author a small haunting using the inherited functions of dead applications; a personally recorded visit can become its guest; the same authored cast produces different, inspectable views for different routes; those bodies, visits and memories survive as the installed desktop.** The integration and execution must earn interest. This is not a world-first or winner-level certification, and the predecessor search is not exhaustive.

## Scene map and narrative escalation

| Moment | Dominant image | Main operation | Persistent consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Prologue — The Last Boot | Sparse recovery console, six dim names, one envelope | Restore/open the fictional session | Cast and invitation become available; no invented timed threat |
| Tableau1 — The Wrong Audience | Warm public video and a separate cold private reflection | Control exposure by staging a real opaque prop | The monster can perform without seeing itself; the actual mask remains |
| Tableau2 — Borrowed Skin | A large illustrated sheet, selection eyes, lifted cut-out and matching absence | Select, extract and compose a body | This exact body/reaction and its source gap recur in show and artifacts |
| Tableau3 — You Did That | A prominent recorded cursor at a real object, one highlighted decision | Change one decision and compare its route consequence | Original/revised visits remain distinguishable; knowledge can differ |
| Tableau4 — Two Invitations, One Secret | Two route-bearing envelopes and returned-view postcards | Distribute access and inspect what was actually shown | Invitations and real observed-view memories remain in the final desktop |
| Connecting farewell — One Last Question | A bounded, overpolite panel resembling a folded curtain | Respond optionally or choose Leave now | The visitor really leaves; no survey gate or new puzzle claim |
| Finale — The Premiere | The same desktop, now inhabited by the saved cast and memories | Save the working show and close TERMINAL | Saved production remains playable without authoring clutter |

The order supports revisiting. The first Visit and explicit unfinished-prefix capture from09 lead to Tableau1's first repair/replay. The body is initially provisional, so no long image-editing tutorial blocks that first reward. Tableau2 then changes the body and reaction, Tableau3 tests a different decision, and Tableau4 makes the difference of access visible. Farewell and final checks lead to the saved premiere. This is not four required chores unlocked by opening program icons.

The recorded-visit option is explicit; the supplied example alternative remains available. Departure-only and unfinished visits retain their real status. The scene presentation cannot turn them into completed performances.

## Tableau1 — The Wrong Audience

### Composition

ECHO occupies the stage as a broad video surface. Its public image has warmth and depth; its small private self-view is colder and more clinical. A provisional CANVAS figure sits in the public image. The eye line of the same ink creature connects ECHO to its own thumbnail.

The dominant visual is a monster frightened of its own reflection. The private and public regions have identifiable boundaries; labels supplement the art. There is no random face photograph or generic jumpscare collage.

### Action and consequence

The player places a declared opaque staged prop over the private region while keeping public footage clear. Replay repeats the genuine entrance prefix; ECHO can now emerge. This is one concise encounter, not a camera puzzle repeated four times. A stable mask remains legitimate throughout the show.

The full reaction supplies the dramatic payoff. With **Collapse**, the authored host comes apart and ECHO is left awkwardly alone. With **Scare back**, that same host turns on ECHO and the supposed monster recoils. Both behaviors work with supported bodies; there is no secret correct silhouette.

The arrangement is the player's state. Transitions cannot move the mask for convenience or uncover a valid private view to manufacture tension. The first saved prefix is still unfinished if no departure was recorded; it does not become a full premiere guest.

## Tableau2 — Borrowed Skin

### Composition

CANVAS temporarily becomes the dominant surface: an illustrated source sheet, its missing patch and a visibly lifted cut-out actor. The eyes belong inside the active selection. The character is large enough to be recognized outside a thumbnail.

Use a prepared illustrated source sheet and a small set of expressive prepared bodies. They are not stock emoji. The player's region, proportion and composition visibly affect the resulting actor, without requiring drawing skill or AI recognition of what it depicts.

### Explicit extension of09: extraction and absence

Before extraction, selection handles show a clearly labeled provisional body preview; they do not yet remove source pixels or change the active staged actor. **Cut a body** confirms that initial selection, installs it as the single host body and shows a matching missing patch in the source image. Once a body is extracted, each confirmed region change atomically updates body and gap; unconfirmed manipulation remains preview. This explicitly refines09's immediate-selection rule. The operation affects only game-provided image data. The original source remains available internally for undo; no real image file is modified.

The source gap, selected pixels/mask and actor are one transaction. Changing the region restores the old source patch before extracting the new one; clearing it restores the source and removes the actor. There are not accumulating duplicate bodies. Undo restores all three together. Selection/body state survives closing or changing the authoring inspector.

The source gap is a hole in the illustrated sheet, with an **opaque window backing** behind it. It does not make the whole staged CANVAS window transparent or silently invalidate camera coverage. The actor's image comes from the retained source patch, not from pixels already erased for the gap.

### Carryover

The body and chosen reaction appear in ECHO, the current poster preview and later visit snapshots. A small source-sheet remnant can remain on the saved desktop with its actual gap; it is not an unrelated final illustration. Previously delivered previews and returned-view postcards preserve their own historical image revision rather than retroactively changing. The installed public invitation must use the final body's poster revision, and Subscriber must complete that exact envelope under the final arrangement. Changing the body invalidates that public-preview check until the invitation is updated and its rehearsal rerun. Historical guest envelopes stay historical and visibly identify their revision.

The scene's question is “What kind of presence will I give this application?” It is not “Which rectangle is the password?”

## Tableau3 — You Did That

### Composition

The recorded visitor occupies the same stage through a clearly labeled violet cursor, object highlight and a narrow strip of action cards. There is no second full timeline editor and no claim to recreate unrecorded mouse movement. The target object is legible at normal viewing scale.

### Action and consequence

Use the existing **Change one decision** operation. For example, the original opens the backstage pass; a revised visit chooses public entry from an envelope that contains both. Backstage reveals anxious preparation without admission. Public entry offers admission and can reveal the staged appearance first.

This contrast is real only when the visits reach different views. A changed preview order that exposes no different information must be reported as equivalent, not celebrated as a new ending.

Preserved suffix cards can become unavailable on the changed route.09's Blocked/Not reached distinctions still apply. Do not retarget them or synthesize actions to complete a demonstration. A compatible supplied visit can demonstrate the contrast if the player declines recording, with clear Example provenance.

The editable original is not silently overwritten. Departures keep their checkpoints; playback never waits through an unwanted performance merely to show a bigger effect. Comparisons identify changes in arrangement, input and received access and do not claim a controlled single-cause experiment if several differ.

## Tableau4 — Two Invitations, One Secret

### Composition

POSTMAN becomes a correspondence table inside the OS: two large envelopes with actual preview, public or backstage attachments. A route attachment opens its declared view rather than merely checking a configuration field. Sending and delivery have a short readable transition, followed by a concrete return artifact.

The author decides which access to supply. A personal or clearly supplied rehearsal can inspect backstage; The Last Subscriber uses the published public route. These are two perspectives on the same show, not two invented human personalities.

### Explicit extension of09: returned-view postcards

Each identified rehearsal can return a postcard rendered from a **scene snapshot actually reached by that visit**. It records the view, relevant event, body revision, arrangement and received envelope. It is a bounded miniature of game scene data, not a screenshot of the user's computer, an AI-generated review or evidence of fear.

Selection is deterministic and labeled: if the visit opened backstage, the default card shows that preparation view; otherwise a completed visible public reaction supplies the public card. A preview-only or interrupted visit gets its actual limited view/status, never a fabricated performance. If there was no qualifying scene view, show a delivery/status record instead of a fictional image.

The scene can juxtapose a backstage preparation card and a public performance card when those events actually occurred. If two visits saw the same state, their cards may match. A card cannot invent a frightened response, gratitude or private knowledge. Opening one identifies and offers to replay its originating visit; the historical card stays frozen even if a later replay uses a revised arrangement.

### Historical invitations remain honest

An original recorded visit keeps its received envelope. Editing or resending cannot revoke its past access. If the author wants to use the recorded decisions with a different invitation, create a clearly identified **new rehearsal copy** with that new envelope; retain the original. Validate every action in the copy and stop at an inaccessible target. This is not an unchanged historical replay or a new personal recording.

For the simplest normal path, the player's saved original already received an optional backstage pass, and the new public invitation goes to Subscriber. No new context copy is required. If the personal route did not use backstage, the player can change a decision using the received valid pass or select the clearly labeled supplied backstage visit. The scene must not force multiple recordings or pretend every person chose a different route.

The returned postcards stay on the installed desktop. POSTMAN's positive reward is seeing and preserving the secret distributed by the invitations. Reports still explain real faults, but seeded broken emails, guessed addresses and required apologies do not drive this scene.

## Farewell and premiere

UNINSTALL has one bounded farewell and an optional answer. Its panels can be staged as a curtain for ECHO's reveal or kept for departure. **Leave now** stays equally available, closes the whole stack and cancels pending cues. Neither the performer nor a replayed visitor is held for a survey. This is a character beat, not another elaborate level.

TERMINAL stores the completed show and says **“You can close me now.”** Closing that console removes the authoring presence and loads the saved initial composition. The actual cast remains in its valid public stage; the private mask stays valid. The body, source remnant, reaction, route attachments and returned postcards remain those created or observed.

The transformation is from **a recovery session containing dead applications** to **a desktop inhabited by their saved production**. It is not a generic castle cutscene or a new gameplay world. Window frames may receive a theatrical art treatment, but production geometry is unchanged. Do not literally remove ECHO's public stage or its mask while claiming the previous visibility rules still hold. Only the confirmed maintenance console is closed for the premiere.

09's completed demonstration guest, Subscriber and two stage-layout conditions still apply. A partial personal prefix stays available in the workshop; it is not certified as a full guest. The provided completed alternative avoids mandatory re-recording.

## Art direction: a nocturnal recovery theatre

### Tokens and hierarchy

| Role | Color | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ink | `#171B20` | Spacious dark desktop and strong silhouettes |
| Bone paper | `#EDE6D8` | Source sheets, cut-out bodies and readable controls |
| Petrol glass | `#305B62` | ECHO's video surface and cooler reflection |
| Aged copper | `#B5794A` | Concentrated warm cues and recovered presence |
| Oxblood | `#7D303A` | Correspondence surface and theatrical depth |
| Recorded violet | `#A696D3` | The one recognizable recorded visitor, paired with a label |

Palette choices are art direction, not a passed accessibility audit. Contrast, selected states and focus need verification in implementation. Body copy uses a readable sans serif; console copy uses a distinct mono face. App names have individual wordmarks inside a common restrained OS chrome. Final font files and third-party assets require a license review before inclusion; no unverified font package is installed here.

### Composition rules

- ECHO is a broad dark image with one isolated reflective thumbnail; CANVAS is a bright vertical source/cut-out relationship; POSTMAN is a diagonal paper composition; REPLAY is a dominant repeated object and a narrow horizontal action strip.
- Keep one operation visually dominant. Preserve common taskbar, program names and real pause/help/exit across scenes. Expanded scene views do not replace the author/visitor/replay mode boundary.
- Use subtly beveled period chrome and graphic paper bodies as the common language. Do not copy a real OS logo or proprietary icon set.
- Introduce character through posture and shape: ECHO is an anxious long-armed ink silhouette, not a random photoreal face. CANVAS's shape is the selected body, not an unrelated mascot swapped into the finale.
- Motion answers an action: a selected patch lifts, a prop covers a reflection, a route attachment opens its view, a postcard arrives. Ambient noise, particles and flicker do not compete constantly with decisions.
- Keep horror legible: no decorative blood, omnipresent pumpkins, permanent VHS distortion, neon dashboard, illegible gothic body copy or surprise fake security warnings.
- Sound supplies optional punctuation. Every causal cue and comedic reversal remains intelligible when muted or with reduced motion.

## Bounded production requirements

Retain the current lightweight2D web direction. No new framework, install, backend service or runtime AI is required by this revision.

The art workload is bounded to one fictional OS shell, four composed scene views, one source-sheet system with a few prepared bodies, one ECHO character with a small reusable pose set, CANVAS's two reaction treatments, two postcard presentation templates and the existing farewell/console framing. Artwork may use layered bitmaps and vector masks. The shared body, visibility, visitor and invitation data drive every scene; do not build a separate rules engine for each.

The baseline excludes six independent campaigns, free-form3D spaces, real OS manipulation, voice production, unrestricted generated narrative, arbitrary image uploads, multiplayer and an endless cast. These limits are intended to keep production manageable for a solo entrant; implementation and artwork estimates remain unverified. The planned variety comes from four operations and composed scenes rather than repeatedly repairing the same mask.

The highest production risks are still meaningful: interactive art can look less polished than a raster illustration, postcard provenance adds saved-state complexity, and replay scene references must remain valid. These require implementation checks; an image generator does not solve them.

## Acceptance for this revised direction

Before calling the built result varied or beautiful, review actual captures and interactions:

1. Without reading captions, the four tableaux have different visual silhouettes. If each capture is a small dark app window plus inspector, revise composition.
2. The player performs construction, exposure control, decision revision and access distribution; four copies of drag-and-cover do not qualify.
3. The authored body and source gap recur faithfully. Replacing them with a default ending character fails the carryover requirement.
4. Two postcards cite actual views and contexts. Identical visits are not artificially made different.
5. The first recognizable replay/consequence precedes advanced editors. Required exposition and travel cannot bury the core effect.
6. The ending keeps the saved production alive while preserving visibility, departure and historical replay contracts.
7. Keyboard, silent play, reduced motion and stage-size checks retain the same decisions; presentation does not hide the real exit.

Additional specified paper cases, not executed software checks:

| Case | Required behavior |
| --- | --- |
| A01 — Preview versus extracted body | Provisional selection does not erase the source or replace the active actor; Cut confirms the initial extraction, and subsequent confirmed region edits update body and gap together |
| A02 — Opaque backing | Source gap does not make CANVAS's staged backing transparent; a valid pinned mask remains valid |
| A03 — Frozen postcard | Editing a reaction after a postcard was received leaves that card unchanged, with its original visit/arrangement identified |
| A04 — Honest public poster | A changed body requires a current public poster and a new Subscriber run; old received envelopes keep their old asset revisions |
| A05 — New invitation copy | Rehearsing original decisions under a new envelope creates a new labeled context and checks route access; no past invitation is silently rewritten |
| A06 — Scene transition | Entering an expanded scene composition or closing its inspector does not move staged masks, delete the actor or add visitor decisions |

This document defines those conditions. It does not report that they passed. A concept board can support an art-direction decision, but cannot establish comprehension, pacing, fun, implementation quality or superiority over winners. The later observation plan in09 remains necessary.
