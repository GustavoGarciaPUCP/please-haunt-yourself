# PLEASE HAUNT YOURSELF — Experience contract

November 6, 2026 · Design baseline for later implementation · No application code or Kiro execution in this revision.

This document closes operational ambiguities in proposal08. It governs the journey, modes, replay, access, visibility, completion and review conditions wherever08 differs. It is a written specification and adversarial review, not implemented behavior, a human usability result or evidence of winning quality. The participant's application-coding stop remains in force.

## 1. The experience we are committing to

**Build a haunted desktop around a replay of your own visit, discover how the same decisions behave in a changed production, and save the dead applications as a playable Halloween show.**

The audience is people who enjoy technology, playful interfaces and short interactive stories. No terminal, audio-production or programming knowledge is required. The intended payoff is recognizing a decision, changing its consequence deliberately and keeping a show that reflects the author's choices.

The six programs remain ECHO, CANVAS, UNINSTALL, REPLAY, POSTMAN and TERMINAL. They contribute to one production. They are not six independent levels, six mandatory tutorials or six full editing applications.

Window occlusion is an introductory compositional rule. It is not described as a deep puzzle or a world-first mechanic. The stronger wager is combining reproducible visitor decisions, authored performances and correspondence that distributes actual routes. That wager still needs an implemented, observed experience.

## 2. One stage, contextual tools

The main surface is the fictional desktop stage. A compact REPLAY transport remains available. One **authoring inspector** opens at a time; selecting CANVAS, POSTMAN or TERMINAL replaces that inspector while preserving edits. TERMINAL's basic view is a short explanation and contextual When/Then rows, not an unrestricted programming graph.

**Staged windows are props, not authoring inspectors.** Multiple staged windows can remain in the composition while the inspector is closed or changed. Closing a working inspector does not remove its corresponding prop. Help, playback controls, the inspector and the real application exit never count as scenery or accidentally cover ECHO's camera.

The first loop uses the scene and playback transport. Reaction links become available when the player asks to change a reaction. Mail appears when choosing who can enter. A visible **Edit show** entry always leads to these tools; later disclosure must not become hidden functionality.

### Player-facing vocabulary

| Visible term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Show | The staged production and the data saved for its premiere |
| Visit | A sequence of visitor decisions |
| Replay | A saved visit being executed against a stated arrangement |
| Edit show | Authoring mode; visitors are not currently acting |
| Record a visit | Explicitly capture only visitor actions inside the fiction |
| Replay of your visit | Persistent label on the violet replay cursor |
| Example visit | Supplied behavior; never attributed to the player |
| The Last Subscriber — simulated visitor | Supplied policy, distinct from a personal recording |

Do not use “take,” “access graph,” “semantic actions,” “policy validation” or “maintenance host” as required player vocabulary.

## 3. Three modes with a clear boundary

| Mode | What the player can do | What the system preserves |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Edit show | Arrange staged windows, construct the body, choose reactions, edit invitations | The current draft; no visitor actions recorded |
| Visit | Interact with received invitation objects and stage controls | Visitor actions only when recording has explicitly started |
| Replay | Observe, pause or inspect the execution of a selected visit | A result tied to that visit, arrangement, envelope and stage size |

**Edit show** during Visit stops recording and asks whether to keep or discard its current sequence. Returning to the workshop is not recorded as a visitor choice. **Edit show** during Replay stops execution and returns actors to the declared starting state before editing. There is no simultaneous live authoring/playback in the baseline.

Recordings describe decisions about declared objects, not mouse trajectories, typing speed, real files or inferred personality. A violet cursor highlights the current target and action; its travel is presentation only. Resizing does not rewrite the recorded decisions.

Recording is optional. **Use an example visit** is available alongside the first recording invitation and later in REPLAY. It supports the full ending, with an explicit Example label. The project demo must distinguish example behavior from actual personal recording. Declining recording does not lock the player out of the show.

**Stop and keep** may preserve an unfinished visit. Ending a recording is not silently converted into the visitor choosing Leave; that visit is marked unfinished until it has a valid ending. Discard removes that personal sequence from the active show and comparisons. Erasing every personal visit switches to the supplied example rather than retaining a hidden “personal” cursor.

## 4. The opening and first reward

### Initial screen copy

**A show for the dead**

“Six deleted apps are still awake. Build them a Halloween show, then save it before closing the console.”

“There is no countdown. This is a fictional desktop: it cannot control your operating system or read your personal files. You can save your show as a local package.”

Primary action: **Record a visit**.

Alternative: **Use an example visit**.

Recording note: “Save the actions you choose inside this rehearsal, then replay them in your show. You can stop or discard the recording.”

### Opening sequence

1. A small, unfinished production already exists: a provisional CANVAS body, an unmasked ECHO private view, an UNINSTALL prop and a valid invitation. The player does not start with an empty editor or a seeded broken email.
2. In Visit mode, the player receives concrete attachments: a poster preview, a public entrance and an optional backstage pass. They can inspect, enter or leave. Entering the public stage and entering from backstage are explicit actions; inspecting never means accepting the performance.
3. During this original Visit, if the player accepts performance admission, ECHO attempts to appear but freezes while seeing its own private image. Its line is **“I can see myself. I can't do this.”** The private thumbnail flickers to ECHO looking back at itself. This is evidence of the condition, not a generic Wrong answer overlay. Recording is still active at this point if the player chose it.
4. The visible options are **Keep this unfinished visit and edit show** and **Leave now**. The first explicitly stops recording and preserves the real entrance prefix without adding a departure; it does not automatically cut the recording at admission. Departure ends and preserves a visit with its actual Leave; explicit Stop elsewhere offers Keep/Discard. After capture has ended, REPLAY shows the real action cards, for example **“You opened the backstage pass.”** The saved visit can now be replayed with its actual status. On the example path there was no recording to stop; Edit show simply stops the supplied replay and keeps it labeled Example.
5. **Show what happened** identifies the private self-view and public stage. It does not initially point to a correct prop position. Optional help can demonstrate an anchor after the player requests a solution-level hint; there is no compulsory hint penalty or time gate.
6. The player edits the composition: an opaque staged window can cover the private panel while the public stage stays clear. Placement updates visibility indicators, but does not automatically perform the show or claim a completed visit.
7. **Replay this unfinished visit** repeats the preserved entrance decisions from the initial state. The changed composition can now permit ECHO's appearance and CANVAS's reaction. At the end of the saved prefix, no visitor action is invented: the house may finish its already-requested response. The result remains **Unfinished visit — no departure recorded**. The intended first reward is **same entrance, different consequence that I caused**, not certification of a complete visit.

If the first visit only inspects and leaves, it remains a correctly completed non-performance visit. Its report says **“You left before the performance.”** It is never forced to enter on replay. If the player enters and chooses Leave while ECHO is blocked, that interruption is also preserved: repairing the scene does not authorize delaying departure until after the show. The player can keep either visit and use an accepted example to rehearse the cast, or voluntarily record another visit. A second personal recording is not mandatory.

The first reward must precede required mail composition, advanced REPLAY editing and reaction-link editing. Exact elapsed time is not an observed result; timing becomes a later usability measurement.

## 5. Decisions after the introductory repair

The workshop exposes three connected authoring decisions. These must produce visible consequences in the saved show, not merely administrative completion ticks.

| Authoring decision | Consequence the visitor can see | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Construct CANVAS's body and choose **Collapse** or **Scare back** | The selected silhouette becomes the host; it falls apart when ECHO appears or turns the scare on ECHO, who recoils | No image-content password; prepared bodies are valid alternatives to drawing |
| Offer public access, optional backstage access, or both | Public visitors meet a staged monster; backstage visitors may first see its unfinished, anxious preparation | Backstage does not itself start the performance; all visits retain a direct return |
| Keep private-view coverage in place or open a staged panel on admission | A stable mask permits an immediate entrance; a modal mask makes its opening part of a comic reveal | Permanent coverage is valid; no invented penalty makes the modal compulsory |

The production is not a challenge to maximize clicks, perfect a scare score or uncover six unrelated passwords. A difficult new editor is not required to create depth. Experimentation is in changing the relationship among entrance, knowledge, composition and reaction.

### Two fully specified productions

**A — “You weren't supposed to see that.”**

The invitation offers a public entrance and poster preview. A staged CANVAS window is pinned over ECHO's private view. A public visitor accepts entry; ECHO appears immediately behind the authored host. With **Collapse**, the host falls apart and ECHO pauses, embarrassed by its overenthusiasm. UNINSTALL then offers its optional last question and an equally available **Leave now**. The show is a concise surprise appearance and farewell.

**B — “Help us scare somebody.”**

The invitation also includes backstage. Before admission, that route exposes ECHO's anxiety and the unfinished set. **Join the show** explicitly accepts performance. Admission opens UNINSTALL's staged panel over the private view; once the panel is visibly in place, ECHO emerges. With **Scare back**, CANVAS turns on the monster and ECHO recoils. The backstage visitor knows what ECHO was trying to do and sees the reversal. Public admission remains supported by the same event, so a visitor need not choose backstage to see the production.

Both public and backstage admission emit the same **Performance entered** event. They retain different arrival context for presentation and optional responses, but the baseline does not require wiring two synonymous entrance triggers. Their exit is independent of route.

These are examples within one system, not two full campaigns or scored difficulty tiers. The information revealed before admission, the order of the reveal and the body's response distinguish them. Swapping only the panel that provides coverage would not demonstrate meaningful depth. No visitor is forced to read backstage material, answer a question or remain until the reversal.

## 6. Visibility and performance rules

The composition defines two visibly separate regions: **Private self-view** and **Public stage**. The private region is the simulated camera image ECHO sees; it is still inspectable by the author. “Private” does not mean a real camera or secret user data.

Only declared opaque scenery masks the private region. The union of its visible opaque surfaces must cover that entire region. A gap, transparent surface, minimized prop or authoring inspector does not provide coverage. Keyboard focus, selected object and coverage are separate states.

The public-stage rectangle must remain unobstructed by opaque scenery for the accepted performance to be certified visible. CANVAS's actor belongs inside the feed and is not treated as a covering desktop window. Hiding the whole call blocks public visibility rather than satisfying both conditions. Reports distinguish **ECHO sees itself** from **The public stage is covered**.

Pins and alignment choices provide equivalent keyboard/click placement. A correctly pinned component keeps its relationship across the two supported stage sizes. A free placement may leave a gap on resize; that exact condition is shown before premiere. No valid arrangement is secretly moved to create a final problem.

Admission creates one pending appearance request. When coverage and public visibility are satisfied, ECHO appears once and CANVAS performs the selected reaction once. If admission opens a masking panel, appearance waits until that panel is in position; it is not lost because the conditions were false at the instant of the click. There is no loop of automatic attempts or repeated jump scares.

If required visibility is lost mid-performance, the performance pauses at that cause and offers editing/replay or departure. It does not complete an invisible reaction. Inspection can explain the condition; it does not move props automatically. Changing the selected image updates the actor in Edit show; changing inspector focus does not erase it. Clearing the selection removes the actor and makes a complete performance unavailable until restored.

The reaction follows appearance. **Leave now** has priority over scheduled or same-step stage reactions: it ends the visit and cancels pending cues. The next replay resets them. A visitor's departure never initiates a final compulsory fright.

The relevant logical checkpoints are before admission, after admission/before appearance, after appearance/before reaction completion, and after the reaction. They exist independently of animation duration. A recorded departure is attached to its checkpoint, so playback does not turn an interrupted show into a full one simply because a mask was repaired or motion was reduced. At an admission checkpoint, a recorded Leave is processed before pending appearance even if coverage is now ready.

## 7. REPLAY without invented behavior

A visit stores ordered decisions referencing an object, action and required phase/access. It also identifies the starting visitor envelope. It preserves a departure's order relative to the observable performance checkpoints, not elapsed seconds or clicking speed. Stage reactions are results, never misrepresented as recorded choices.

If a required checkpoint cannot be reached, stop at the earlier failed condition and name it rather than waiting indefinitely or inventing a later departure. The real Stop/exit controls remain available. If a partial recording reaches its end without Leave, do not append a visitor decision: let already-requested stage responses finish or report their blocking condition, and retain **Unfinished — no departure recorded**. A complete visible reaction alone does not make this a complete visit.

REPLAY selects visits and handles their decisions. TERMINAL explains the house's response. POSTMAN determines received routes. They are not three editing timelines.

### Re-recording one decision

The visible action is **Change one decision**, not a required film-production term. Replay the prefix under the selected arrangement, pause before the chosen decision and let the player perform one currently available replacement action. Keep the original visit and the replacement as distinguishable versions. Preserve the following action cards rather than rewriting them to make the new visit work.

Before each following action, check the recipient, phase and received access. If an object exists but is unavailable on that route, mark the first impossible block **Blocked — this object isn't available here** and pause. Offer **Undo decision change**, **Change this decision** or **Edit show**; never silently skip it, retarget it or open a forbidden route.

**Departure is a distinct terminal case:** if the new action is Leave, the visit ends successfully. The retained suffix is visible as **Not reached — visit ended**. Those unexecuted cards are not a failed exit and cannot reopen the ended visit. Such a recording does not certify a full performance.

### Comparisons that can support a causal claim

- **Same visit, changed arrangement:** use the identical sequence and received envelope at the same stage size, with the original and revised arrangements. Identify all changed settings. If more than one setting changed, do not claim to have isolated a single cause.
- **Changed decision, same arrangement:** use the original and revised sequence, identical received access, stage size and initial cast state.

Each run starts with reset actor states, pending cues, modal stack and visitor phase. Mail already received by that simulated visitor is not silently revoked by clearing visual history. Comparisons use declared fresh rehearsals and the identified envelope. Results name the performed action, condition and visible consequence, not a score for fear, personality or intelligence.

## 8. POSTMAN and the second visitor

POSTMAN distributes bounded playable attachments:

- **Preview:** the authored poster/body image; opens without starting a show.
- **Public entrance:** offers explicit public admission.
- **Backstage pass:** opens a preparation view and a separate **Join the show** action.

**Return is always available. It is not an attachment the author must remember to send.** UNINSTALL can stage a farewell inside the production, but **Leave now** must close the entire staged stack. The real pause/help/exit controls are outside the fiction and remain usable.

The starting invitation works. There is no mandatory seeded mismatch, secret email address or required apology. Optional invitation text does not change behavior through language-model interpretation. The preview displays the actual selected body. Delivery has an inspectable attachment summary, so choosing what a visitor receives is a concrete authoring act.

Received envelopes are immutable records. Editing a draft, deleting a sent item or sending a correction does not change an old recipient's access. **Send a new rehearsal invitation** creates an identified new envelope/visit context. The baseline needs one invitation, correction/reply and inspectable sent/received records; elaborate forwarding chains and an unrestricted mail client are outside scope.

### The Last Subscriber — visible simulated route

Before running this visitor, show its plan:

1. Open the received Preview; if absent or it launches performance, report that mismatch.
2. Use the Public entrance and explicitly accept admission. Do not inspect Backstage on the way. Preview is the poster/body image; backstage is the different preparation view, not an undefined extra portrait action.
3. Observe the requested appearance and selected host reaction.
4. At the farewell, choose **Leave now** without answering UNINSTALL's question.

If an access required by this plan is absent, name it and stop that run. Do not invent a different route. If a reaction depends only on optional backstage inspection, public admission exposes that missing response. The player can use **Performance entered** as its trigger. The visitor never evaluates arbitrary prose or whether the scene is actually scary.

A backstage-only private invitation can remain saved as an alternative, but it is not evidence that the supplied public visitor completed the show. The **public invitation for the installed show** includes preview and public entrance; backstage is optional. A replayed personal guest separately retains its own immutable received envelope, even if that earlier invitation was backstage-only. The saved package includes both identified contexts. Never replace a guest's received access with the new public invitation. That personal visit must still be executable against the final arrangement, or the player can repair it or select a compatible example. No new personal recording is required.

The simulation is a second known path, not proof of general robustness or human enjoyment. A personal visit can happen to match this path; the system must not pretend it observed a difference that did not occur.

## 9. Contextual reactions, recovery and completion

TERMINAL's initial reactions are connected: performance admission requests ECHO, appearance triggers the selected CANVAS reaction, and Leave releases the visitor. First repair is compositional. Later editing a response opens one plain-language When/Then row beside the involved prop. The system exposes its result before offering additional rows. Basic links can be restored without rebuilding the production.

UNINSTALL is a character with a bounded farewell and optional response, not a blocker that must consume clicks. Public and backstage admission use one baseline event, so no author has to discover a hidden synonym just to make the second visitor work.

There are four distinct visit results:

| Result | Meaning | What it proves |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Left before performance | Departure without admission | Entry was not forced; return worked |
| Left during performance | Admission, then departure before the reaction finished | Return worked; a whole performance was not demonstrated |
| Completed performance and left | Admission, visible ECHO, visible chosen CANVAS reaction, then an actually executed departure | A full show and completed visit on that identified arrangement/size; a merely available exit is insufficient |
| Needs repair / unfinished | Unavailable recipient, missing condition or a recording stopped before completion | The named condition remains; no completed show is claimed |

Saving the draft is always possible. **Save show and premiere** requires:

- A selected, completed demonstration visit: personal or clearly labeled example. A personal unfinished prefix or early-exit visit may be kept alongside it and remains replayable in the workshop. The personal prefix used for the first reward is not falsely certified as a complete premiere guest; a compatible example avoids forcing another personal recording.
- At least one complete accepted performance and departure under the final arrangement.
- The Last Subscriber completing its declared public route under that arrangement.
- Both supported stage sizes satisfying the visibility conditions and working direct departure. This is a limited size/route check, not a claim of support for every browser or device.
- Actual body selection, reaction, staged layout, supplied invitations, selected visits and links included in the saved production.

Changing a relevant setting marks dependent rehearsal evidence **Needs rehearsal**. Old results remain identifiable as history and cannot certify the new arrangement. A complete show is not unlocked by counting edits, opening all six apps or personalizing a name.

Closing TERMINAL early offers **Keep editing** or **Save draft and leave**. There is no countdown and no destructive punishment. The fictional cast remains in its temporary session until a completed show is saved; draft recovery preserves the user's work without pretending that a premiere occurred.

## 10. The ending rewards this production

TERMINAL saves the cast into the declared interactive theme and says **“You can close me now.”** Closing it then loads the saved show from its declared initial state, not from whichever windows or transformed actors remained after the last rehearsal.

The premiere uses the actual body, reaction, coverage, invitation, route and selected visitor. A public invitation produces the surprise entrance; an included backstage pass remains a working alternative. There is no generic unrelated ending illustration.

The installed public invitation and a replay guest's historical envelope have different roles: new visits use the installed invitation; the selected replay uses its own preserved context, as checked before saving. The premiere identifies which visit is playing. A recorded backstage guest is not silently converted into a public entrant.

CANVAS stays the host. A recorded visitor does not acquire a new, undefined host personality. The completed premiere visit uses its recorded departure. Afterward, **Replay this guest again** and **Return to edit show** keep the saved production usable. Original partial and early-exit visits remain in the workshop library with their actual results; none is rewritten into departure, gratitude or cooperation.

The player can keep editing and replaying after the ending. The bounded local show package preserves fictional scene data and references; it is not a real OS theme, arbitrary website export or executable attachment. Import validation and save compatibility remain implementation work; no functioning export is claimed yet.

## 11. Presentation and access decisions

All product text is English. The aesthetic contrast is an orderly recovery desktop whose period applications become performers: cold console text, a warm failing video image, a visibly selected paper-like body and overpolite farewell panels. The palette uses charcoal, bone, amber and restrained violet for the recorded visitor; each program has a readable name and silhouette. Decorative glitching must not obscure controls or mimic a real system security alert.

The visual emphasis follows one decision at a time: the received object, the replayed object, then the condition affecting the stage. Dialogue is a short reaction at the relevant moment, with optional transcript/history. No required exposition scroll precedes the first replay.

Body selection, pins, attachment choice, replay and contextual links work with keyboard and click alternatives. Sound is optional punctuation, never a clue. Reduced motion preserves sequence and meaning without fast glitches, compulsory camera movement or a timed response. Text/icons accompany state colors. The staged modal stack cannot trap real focus outside the always-available controls.

The baseline targets desktop/laptop browsers with two declared logical stage layouts: **Wide,960×600**, and **Compact,720×600**. The inspector sits outside the evaluated stage and can move below it. Pins follow the named private/public regions across layouts. These are scene coordinates, not a claim that only two real screen resolutions work. Narrower screens can show a fitted stage with the same controls, but full mobile ergonomics is not claimed by these checks.

No image uploads, microphone, camera, unrestricted generated dialogue, real process management or external account integration is required. Prepared shapes and local artwork are sufficient for the baseline's authoring depth.

## 12. Adversarial paper checks and evidence limits

The following are specified expected outcomes from a document review, not executed application tests or observed participant responses.

| Case | Setup/action | Required outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P01 — Author/visitor boundary | Change CANVAS in Edit show; record an invitation visit | Body is initial scene data, not a recorded visitor action; inspector never supplies camera coverage |
| P02 — Early departure | Inspect preview; Leave before admission | No ECHO appearance, no survey gate; completed non-performance visit retained |
| P03 — Stable coverage | Pin a staged CANVAS window; replay both stage sizes | Correct coverage remains correct; public feed remains visible; no scripted sabotage |
| P04 — Re-record to Leave | Replace Enter with Leave while retaining later modal action cards | Visit ends; later cards show Not reached; no forced re-entry and no failed-exit result |
| P05 — Other unavailable suffix | Change route so a later object's access is absent before departure | Pause at the first unavailable action, name its access/phase, preserve original and revised visits |
| P06 — Old mail/new mail | Receive backstage; delete sent record; send public-only correction | Old envelope retains backstage; new run declares its different envelope, never silently mutates the old one |
| P07 — Inspector versus prop | Close CANVAS inspector while its pinned staged window remains | Body selection and stage mask persist; editing chrome never alters performance geometry |
| P08 — Public path | Author response depends on optional backstage inspection; run Subscriber | Public route reports missing response; no fabricated backstage action; shared admission can repair it |
| P09 — Exit priority | Request Leave as a modal/appearance cue is pending | End visit and cancel cues before the appearance; late reactions cannot frighten someone already outside |
| P10 — Bad coverage | Hide whole call, leave one private gap, or use translucent prop | Explain public obstruction or private exposure accurately; neither produces a certified appearance |
| P11 — Recording declined | Use example, construct show, complete supplied visitor | Full ending works; provided sequence remains labeled Example; no hidden personal recording appears |
| P12 — Save and restart | End a rehearsal with open panels/transformed actors; save and premiere | Load saved initial scene and actual chosen reactions/routes; no transient rehearsal residue or later unsaved edits |
| P13 — Permanent mask | Complete all required paths with a stable CANVAS mask | Accept it; no forced UNINSTALL mask, arbitrary resource penalty or false strategic-depth claim |
| P14 — Evidence invalidation | Change entry response after a completed rehearsal | Mark relevant result Needs rehearsal; retain historical result; do not certify the changed production |
| P15 — Partial recording | Stop recording before leaving or completing a reaction | Preserve unfinished visit if requested; do not invent Leave or certify a full show |
| P16 — Historical guest/public invitation | Complete a backstage guest, later send a public premiere invitation, select the guest | Guest keeps its checked historical envelope; new public visitors use the installed envelope; both contexts are saved and identified |
| P17 — Repaired interruption | Record Enter then Leave before appearance; repair private coverage and replay | Recorded Leave still precedes appearance and cancels it; do not delay it or certify a full performance |
| P18 — Genuine first reward | Record entrance; explicitly Keep unfinished visit and edit show; repair mask; replay prefix | Same entrance can now produce appearance/reaction; do not append Leave; label visit unfinished and use a completed personal/example guest for premiere |
| P19 — Missing checkpoint | A recording leaves after the reaction, but the revised show cannot produce an appearance | Stop at the named blocking condition; do not wait forever, fabricate the reaction or silently move Leave earlier |

### What this review closed

The creative and adversarial agents exchanged objections directly. A separate cold-reading agent reviewed the proposed player copy and mode boundaries without the project history. Codex resolved their conflicts in this contract. This is multi-agent document critique, not three humans playing.

Resolved design errors include: early Leave causing a later performance; treating a return attachment as necessary for escape; inspector windows supplying unintended coverage; equivalent exposure triggers requiring duplicate wiring; preserved suffix actions reopening an ended visit; a recorded guest becoming an undefined host; and a permanent mask falsely presented as a deep strategic challenge.

The design now specifies modes, opening, decisions, two productions, replay semantics, access, visibility, failure/recovery and an ending tied to saved choices. Additional applications and subsystems are out of scope for this baseline. Later implementation may expose contradictions that require a recorded revision rather than silent reinterpretation.

### What cannot be closed by more agent agreement

Recognition, comprehensibility, amusement, pacing and desire to experiment require observation. When an interactive version is authorized, use a short independent session without coaching the first attempt:

1. Observe whether a person can identify what the replay repeated, using a concrete object/action rather than a vague “it copied me.”
2. Before a second replay, ask what consequence they expect from their chosen change; compare the explanation with the visible result.
3. Ask for a second production that changes what the visitor learns or how the cast reacts, without dictating which tool to use.
4. Observe a direct departure and check that the person distinguishes leaving correctly from completing a performance.
5. Ask what the saved premiere preserves; verify that the replayed outcome corresponds to those choices.
6. After the required activity ends, allow a voluntary choice to stop or try another arrangement. Do not force another attempt and count compliance as engagement.

These are diagnostic questions, not guaranteed numerical thresholds. Failures reopen the relevant design decision: mode confusion changes labels/boundaries; an unexplained repair changes feedback; mere obedient copying changes the authoring choice; boredom calls for revising/removing the weak beat. More effects or programs are not the default remedy. Do not substitute a provided simulated visitor for this later human observation.

## 13. Basis and sources consulted November 6, 2026

The following established sources informed design decisions; they are not publications newly released in2026 and do not guarantee competition success.

- Jakob Nielsen, [Progressive Disclosure](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/),2006: defer advanced controls while making the route to them visible. Applied here to contextual authoring rather than six initial editors.
- Aurora Harley, [Visibility of System Status](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/visibility-system-status/),2018: communicate state and feedback so a user can assess actions. Applied to recording/replay mode, private/public visibility and named failed conditions.
- Raluca Budiu, [Memory Recognition and Recall in User Interfaces](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/recognition-and-recall/),2024: provide recognizable objects and available actions. Applied to recorded object references, persistent replay labels and contextual choices rather than memorized commands.
- Clara Fernandez-Vara, [Puzzle Writing: Best Practices](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1013851/puzzle-writing-best), GDC Online2010: the published session overview connects sufficient puzzle information, world coherence and narrative progress. Applied to causal clues and app-specific consequences. Only the public overview was consulted; no claim to have watched the full lecture.
