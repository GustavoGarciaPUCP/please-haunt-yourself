## Original prompt

> Use case: ui-mockup / illustrated narrative storyboard.
> Create one polished six-panel VISUAL CONCEPT BOARD for an original fictional Halloween desktop browser game titled "PLEASE HAUNT YOURSELF". This is a proposed art direction, NOT screenshots of working software. Wide landscape board, 3 columns by 2 rows, generous precise gutters, each panel a different screen composition of the SAME fictional operating system. View screens straight on; no monitor hardware or perspective photos. Put the exact main title above the panels, and the smaller subtitle "Visual concept — not gameplay". All visible copy English. Panel captions must be readable: "The Last Boot", "The Wrong Audience", "Borrowed Skin", "You Did That", "Two Invitations", "The Premiere".
> Art direction: a nocturnal theatre of resurrected software. Original early desktop chrome rendered with care: subtly beveled pewter titlebars, tactile bone paper, restrained dark petrol video glass, oxblood theatre shadows and aged copper highlights. Not an exact Windows clone. Consistent small taskbar and six app names across screens: ECHO, CANVAS, UNINSTALL, REPLAY, POSTMAN, TERMINAL. Restrained ghost-violet only for the recorded visitor cursor. Strong 2D graphic compositions, crafted cut-paper creatures, elegant negative space, illustration quality fit for an indie game art book, beautiful yet achievable with layered bitmap art and SVG. Visual depth by overlapping flat layers and ink texture, NOT cinematic 3D worlds. No gore, pumpkins pasted everywhere, stock haunted mansion, random neon, green hacker dashboard, opaque blurry text, or identical cards.
> Recurring cast: ECHO is a distinctive shy long-armed soot-black ink monster with two small cream eyes and a worried expressive posture. CANVAS's host is a pale paper-cut cloaked figure with drawn eyes, whose body comes from an image selection. Consistency matters. Use the same silhouettes later; the host can become more developed after its body-making panel.
> Panel 1, The Last Boot: almost empty, ink-black fictional desktop. One small ivory console with a few short lines; six dim app icons like an unlit marquee and one physical-looking invitation envelope. Concentrated warm console light, no wall of code. This is the quiet opening, not a puzzle.
> Panel 2, The Wrong Audience: a big amber-tinted fictional video window dominates. The paper host in the PUBLIC stage on the left, shy ink monster behind it. A clearly separate small PRIVATE self-view at upper right shows the same monster looking nervously at itself. One opaque ivory staged farewell panel is placed over the private thumbnail; preserve the public stage unobstructed. Show the spatial relation cleanly, not many contradictory windows. Small violet replay cursor indicates a recorded admission object. The visual joke is the monster being scared of seeing itself, not a generic jumpscare.
> Panel 3, Borrowed Skin: CANVAS fills the desktop with a large illustrated sheet. A dashed selection surrounds a patch shaped as a papery cloak creature. The cut-out body is lifted next to a conspicuous matching hole left in the source image. Eyes live inside the selected patch. A few practical selection handles, no big tool dashboard. Rich ivory on ink, tactile cut edges, one elegant copper seam. The physical absence left behind must be obvious.
> Panel 4, You Did That: the SAME haunted stage with the now-authored paper host and ink monster, but composed around a prominent violet recorded cursor on a real backstage-pass object. A narrow strip of only three legible action cards indicates replay, with one selected card. A small comparison vignette shows entering via the public doorway versus seeing the worried monster backstage. No fake emotions score, no multiple anonymous ghost characters. It is an inspectable recorded visitor and an actual route difference, not a split-screen video editor.
> Panel 5, Two Invitations: large diagonal paper envelopes on an oxblood desk-like desktop area. One backstage pass, one public entrance; each is an actual pictured route attachment rather than a form field. Two returned postcards show the SAME cast from different real views: the monster anxious in preparation, and the public performance where the paper host scares the monster back. Postcards include subtle tiny route labels only. Distinctive paper composition with strong diagonal silhouettes and generous space. No invented review stars or psychological analysis.
> Panel 6, The Premiere: the SAME fictional desktop now visibly inhabited and warm. The paper host and ink monster perform inside the preserved public video/stage; former software chrome has become a neat theatrical frame, with the private-view mask still physically valid. The two postcards sit offstage. The small console is closing after a compact visible saved-state indication, revealing the actual staged composition. The scene must preserve the prior body and props rather than becoming a fantasy landscape. The contrast with the sparse boot panel is the payoff.
> Finish: precise consistent art, all panels fully within canvas, clear silhouette variety from panel to panel, readable minimal English text, no watermark. This board establishes a target to critique, not a claim of completed game UI.

## First-output inspection

The first board established consistent paper/ink characters and a common OS/theatre language. It also had material inconsistencies: the private preview remained exposed in performance frames, ECHO was mislabeled as REPLAY, video timing/scrubbers implied a recording model we do not use, a farewell invented a recorded departure, and repeated stage/audience compositions weakened variety. These are defects in the illustration, not approved product features. A targeted revision is requested below.
