## What is materially better

- The model attributes the latest action and separates an answered summons (`recalled`) from waiting at the door (`threshold`). This closes a real cause/feedback ambiguity.
- Changing the note/whisper/bell does not overwrite the ghost's semantic position. The scene and shell use one state projection.
- The default room uses Halloween objects, plain language and optional technical inspection. Hushing, memory choice, return, residue and clean release are actually different states.
- The bell tally and tab indicator connect the haunting to a familiar application affordance. Both outcomes still change the shell and inspectable note.

This is stronger evidence than a cosmetic reskin. It is still not human enjoyment testing, a complete multi-application game or proof of global originality.

## Product judgment

The next jump should be from a well-illustrated control panel toward a short complete playable story. More buttons, effects, programs or backstory alone will not achieve that. The first encounter needs a legible invitation, one recognizable surprise, experimentation with attributable consequences and an ending that returns a genuinely changed tool to the player.

Proposed audience: people who enjoy short Halloween browser experiences, including people who do not program. Proposed value: enter a familiar application that behaves strangely, understand a repeating cause through play, choose what to retain and see that application recover. This is positioning to test, not a market-research result or a proven educational outcome. Preserve English product text and do not recast the project as a full course or production OS.

My strongest direction is progressive discovery and an authored handover: let the player witness the ghost's failed farewell, explore the relevant objects without a control wall, and reclaim a functioning prompt at the end. A small personal first line could make that handover tangible, but Opus should compare it with alternatives and reject it if it adds a form without improving the experience. Keep/fade must remain legitimate, distinguishable choices, not a moral quiz.
