## Critical assessment

The previous brief had a causal two-app hypothesis but too little change in the desktop as a whole. A room, some better controls and state-aware dialogue can still feel static. The missing layer is an understandable dramatic progression: the player should see what their intervention changed at the scale of the fictional computer, then receive a payoff shaped by their own actions.

A fog layer and a hand are recognizable horror imagery, not an innovation claim. The stronger opportunity is their interaction with real movable windows, the source ECHO is inspecting and an artifact made during this playthrough. An effect that could be placed after any unrelated task is insufficient. More blur, more darkness and more interruptions would make the experience worse.

## Proposed major scene: the ghost reaches the glass

**Rule to evaluate:** the same apparition can change its presentation surface when the player breaks its repeating call. It starts inside TERMINAL. A genuine partial repair allows it to reach the fictional desktop's glass. This is visible progress, not a new villain or the old puzzle secretly restarting.

The screen means the browser game's viewport. It does not mean the real monitor, browser chrome, operating system or another application's windows.

### A concrete sequence

1. **Pressure before the reveal.** While the call is active, a faint condensation edge accompanies the relevant bell event. It does not run as a random wallpaper loop. The app's own output records the cause, so audio is unnecessary.
2. **A real milestone.** Once the player actually breaks the repeat, TERMINAL confirms it immediately: `The call has stopped.` The doorway opens or its equivalent makes the repair clear. The existing interaction cannot merely reset after the celebration.
3. **The viewport changes.** Condensation gathers across the fictional glass; the visual treatment suggests a cold night outside and breath on the other side. A silhouette passes and a hand presses against it. The working controls remain readable and usable. A global opaque blur of the whole application is not the art direction.
4. **The spatial discovery.** Moving TERMINAL or ECHO exposes the handprint, but the print stays on the desktop glass. The window follows the pointer normally; the trace does not travel with it. Its coordinate space communicates something the player can test: the presence is no longer inside that window. A keyboard move/reveal alternative exposes the same fact.
5. **A human-scale interaction.** The player can wipe an exposed area of condensation. The presence answers on the other side of that cleared area, perhaps opening its palm rather than slamming the screen. This gesture changes the discoverable surface and retained image; no reflex test, precise drawing or hidden shape is required.
6. **The two apps have work to do.** TERMINAL can confirm what request remains; ECHO can compare the relevant sources. A capture of the now-empty terminal should not falsely contain a glass-level apparition. If ECHO can capture the glass, that source must exist explicitly in the game's shared scene model. The player can understand why a window capture and a surface capture differ.
7. **A personal result.** The eventual still can retain the actual cleared region, the selected presence state and the source used. A hand touching the place the player cleared is a stronger farewell than the same pre-rendered jump scare every time. ECHO shows/exports that actual retained image; the desktop clears and ordinary app behavior remains available.

This sequence is a candidate to improve or replace with a better causal scene. It must not become a new arbitrary Capture -> Finish gate. Opus must explain which actions resolve an obligation, which only expose evidence and which shape the retained result. A narrative reveal is not automatically a second puzzle.

## Three creative additions from Codex

### 1. A displaced trace makes window movement meaningful

The requested dragging is also a way to discover that an anomaly belongs to a different surface. Use one persistent handprint or shadow, not a swarm of independently lagging windows. Do not delay the actual window drag to make it spooky. It must remain precise, and moving windows must never become another walk-across-windows platformer.

**Failure condition:** if the player has no reason to move a window or cannot expose the trace using keyboard/arrange controls, the clue is accidental. Provide an observable invitation such as a partial print crossing a window edge, not a tooltip that recites the solution.

### 2. Each program witnesses progress in its own language

TERMINAL adds a short relevant line to its output; ECHO's live/recorded evidence shows the same event; a future CANVAS encounter could retain a chosen revealed mark as a layer; a future POSTMAN receipt could acknowledge the actual attached artifact. Each app has a distinct presentation and capability, rather than all four displaying the same spooky toast.

Suggested TERMINAL line after the surface change: `The call has stopped. Something reached the glass.` This confirms success and points to the new observation. Do not blame the player for repairing the program or undo the progress to manufacture more play.

**Failure condition:** lines that claim actions which did not happen, fake delivered receipts, or a future app being necessary for today's two-app ending.

### 3. The last scene contains what this player made

Plan a quiet final desktop where the retained still, optional later drawing and actual later receipt form a small record of this session. For this two-app iteration, use only implemented artifacts. The final hand gesture can mirror the cleared region or selected retained image, without pretending to infer the player's personality or feelings.

The payoff can reinterpret an initially threatening palm as a response or goodbye. It should still read as Halloween, with no requirement to add a tragic biography. The normal cursor and usable apps afterward make the repair tangible. If the player chooses a no-keepsake ending, acknowledge that choice honestly instead of displaying an artifact they never retained.

**Failure condition:** a fixed congratulations overlay that looks the same regardless of the retained image, or a successful ending that immediately starts the same haunting again without an explicit replay choice.

## Dramatic progression across the planned four programs

| Phase | What visibly changes | Player meaning | Scope now |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Presence inside a program | Ghost output, one motivated bell return and source evidence | Something has a repeatable cause I can investigate | Implement in TERMINAL/ECHO |
| Progress reaches the shared desktop | The call stops; condensation and a displaced handprint appear | My intervention changed where the presence can be observed | Implement one complete causal global scene |
| Other programs contribute different operations | A drawing contains chosen marks; a thread yields a real local receipt | Recovered tools can do different work in one world | Specify CANVAS/POSTMAN or better alternatives; do not report them as implemented |
| Release and a usable desktop | The surface clears; actual retained artifacts and restored app functions remain | The outcome reflects what I did and what I chose to keep | Complete the current two-app ending; plan the four-app culmination |

The expansion cannot be four versions of fog, four bells, four capture buttons or four mandatory tutorials. Plan major reveals at meaningful changes of state; routine sub-actions need immediate small feedback, not a full-screen interruption.

## Choreography proposal

### Art direction: wet glass, not a generic blur

Keep the existing charcoal/petrol/bone/copper desktop identity. Use a restrained cold grey-blue in the condensation and a trace of the existing warm copper as reflected light; do not replace every app's identity with a neon-green horror skin. Condensation should read as uneven water density, small cleared streaks and diffuse contact edges, not smoke particles or a uniform frosted rectangle.

The silhouette is low-contrast and subordinate. The hand is the focal object: readable anatomy, softly diffused palm, clearer fingertip/contact areas and a shape that feels pressed against the same surface the player can wipe. Match the hand's illustration/rendering style to the game. A floating hand icon, pumpkin emoji or enlarged existing sprite is not a substitute for composing this scene.

Choose a restrained illustrated treatment if that yields a more coherent result than an unconvincing photorealistic attempt. No new runtime asset generator is required. Review the effect at actual PC size, not only in a cropped close-up. It should communicate depth and touch in a silent still while leaving the apps readable. Judge condensation, anatomy, contact and composition separately from whether the animation code runs.

### Timing

Provisional timing for the first surface reveal; tune it from an actual recording. This rare narrative sequence may last around two seconds. The product UI remains interactive from the beginning. Its normal repeated actions do not inherit the cinematic timing.

### What does not move, and why

- Window controls and taskbar: orientation and leaving the scene must remain reliable.
- The app window during dragging: direct pointer positioning, without cinematic lag.
- Main action labels and body text: legibility is more valuable than another animated layer.
- The settled handprint: it is inspectable evidence, so it does not vanish before the player understands it.

### Feel check

Code cannot prove whether the silhouette reads as pressure behind glass, whether the palm feels like a presence rather than an icon, or whether the final response feels connected to the player's gesture. Review a recording around the silhouette-to-hand overlap and around a window exposing the fixed trace. Also review one still without sound and the reduced-motion presentation. Reject the scene if the player can only describe it as an unrelated overlay after a success message.

## Interaction and rendering contract

- Do not blur the application's whole root or hide its primary controls. Use a separately owned visual surface with legibility-aware masks/opacity. Keep exit/skip/hide controls reachable; a cinematic must not take over the operating system or require actual full-screen mode.
- The visual surface is not a third program. Reuse the existing browser-native stack; a short SVG/CSS/canvas composition may be sufficient. No new framework, host screenshot API, camera permission or runtime AI is required for the candidate.
- Define the object's identity, current surface, request state, exposed region and capture result in one shared model. Rendering the hand is not what commits progress. ECHO and the global view must not invent independent hands or return events.
- Specify what each ECHO source contains. Export a composite from the same scene data, with the actual mask/selected state. Do not silently substitute a stock image or claim to capture the real screen. Source selection should be obvious enough to investigate, not a hidden correct-answer menu.
- Store positions/marks in a coordinate system that remains valid across the target PC sizes. A window-relative object and a glass-relative trace must not drift together after resize. Cap/simplify gesture data so the retained artifact does not grow indefinitely.
- Wiping cannot steal pointer capture from titlebar drag or activate underneath buttons. It needs explicit ownership and a keyboard/button equivalent that reveals the same evidence. It must not require fine motor control or completion of a fixed traced shape.
- Reduced motion presents static condensation/hand evidence and the same actions. Sound is optional; important facts survive with sound off. No rapid flashes or enforced volume spikes.
- If interrupted by close, minimize, scene exit, Undo, replay/reset or changed motion preference, cancel the presentation cleanly. Preserve committed progress. Resume with inspectable evidence rather than replaying a major scare every time.
- A queued reveal must not play into a hidden tab or keep mutating gameplay from animation timers. Define when its presentation is consumed, how reload/migration restores it and how explicit replay differs from accidental remount.
